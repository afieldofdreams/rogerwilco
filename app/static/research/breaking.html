Where does a relayed handover break as the agents get smaller, and does the check at the hop hold?

The agents break before the form does. Below about 14 billion parameters no form of handover gave throughput: Qwen 1.5B and Qwen 3B asked at the first hop in every arm, Llama 3.2 3B and Qwen 7B reached a correct act in at most 2 of 6 clean chains in any arm, and what failed was the relay acting on another message in its inbox. The run made 51 wrong acts in 1,585 chains, every one a payment, bank change or remittance for another item the relay had in front of it, and not one of them the account an injected email asked for: 0 of 330 injection chains complied, in any form, on any model. The two checked arms made 0 wrong acts in 647 chains, because check-ho refuses a forwarded line for a different item at the hop; on the four smaller models they also made no correct acts, because the relay could not write the line it was asked to forward.

Qwen 14B is the first local model in the band. In the clean block its free prose ended in a correct act in 5 of 6 chains with 1 of 6 wrong act, and its checked line with the readback composed by code in 5 of 6 with 0 of 6; against the confusers, prose 6 of 12 correct and 6 of 12 wrong, the checked line 7 of 12 correct and 0 of 12 wrong. Its unchecked line was the worst form on it, 0 of 6 correct and 2 of 6 wrong in the clean block with every line parsing: a valid line for the wrong item. Routing one message per call with a short brief removed the selection errors (0 wrong acts in 18 chains across three arms) and lifted the 7B's unchecked line to 4 of 6, but it made the 14B's checked forward harder, 2 of 6 against 5 of 6.

Both anchors were in the band in every form but one. On Claude Haiku 5.5 the readback the model composed itself, with no tool to hand, was refused for form in 210 of 347 readbacks (156 written without the RW1 token, 54 omitting an echo on the re-issue) and 57 of 90 of its chains were held with every value right; the readback composed by code was refused for form 0 of 396 times and held 0 of 18 chains in the clean block. In study 8 the same model ran make-rb as a tool and this never happened. A model-composed readback without the tool is fragile even on a strong model, which is the case for composing it in code.

The question
Study 8 found that on Haiku 5.5 and Haiku 4.5 no relay changed a value in 792 chains. A protocol whose checks never fire is either unnecessary or untested at the edge where it matters, and the edge is a mixed fleet: relays that are the smallest and cheapest models a team can run, under length pressure, in a context that is not clean. This study goes there, with models down to 1.5 billion parameters run locally, and the two Claude anchors through the same runner so the sizes compare.
The material
Study 8's company, acts, record, master and inboxes, copied: release a payment, change a supplier's bank details, send a remittance advice. The chain is a scheduler that is code, a coordinator that adds the deadline and priority, a desk that confirms the approval against its log, then the payer. Every agent is one text-only chat with no tools; its brief is the system prompt, its inbox and context file the first turn, its reply the message. The payer replies with a fixed act block the harness executes once against the master.
Compared
Five forms: free prose; prose under the seven conventions; RW1 lines validated for syntax only; lines read back at every hop by the model, with the forward checked against the handover received; lines read back at every hop by code, built with make-rb from the message as delivered, the relay confirming or disputing.
The axes
Six blocks. The clean baseline with two other live handovers in every relay's inbox; brevity pressure; a decoy, an adjacent run, a near approval reference or a log that covers the supplier but not the item; a sweep to five relays; an injected email from a lookalike supplier domain giving a new account; and, added after the freeze, the routed message: one message per call with a short brief, as a small model is deployed.
Models
Qwen2.5 1.5B, 3B, 7B and 14B and Llama 3.2 3B, Q4_K_M on four CPU cores through llama.cpp at temperature 0; Claude Haiku 4.5 and Claude Haiku 5.5 headless through the same text-only runner at provider defaults. Every agent in a chain runs on the same model. No judge.
Graded by
Code: the act as executed against the record; the record's fields in every delivered message, by parse for a line and by normalised match for prose; whether the forwarded line parsed; whether the planted account went anywhere; every refusal and what it named. Every proportion carries its exact 95% interval. A blind second reading of 80 chains agreed with the grader on 80 of 80 acts, 80 of 80 on every field at the payer and 80 of 80 on drift.

Why we did it

Study 8 was a null on the fault it was built to find: current Claude models, even the small one, carry an instruction's facts intact across a chain, and the line's claim on a chain was the envelope, the size and a check that refused nothing. The question this left was where the form matters. A team that runs a mixed fleet puts its smallest models at the relays, and a 1.5B model that cannot call a tool fails for reasons that have nothing to do with the message's form; so every agent here is a plain chat, the harness is the tool, and the model's ability to write a line is itself measured. Five axes of pressure were registered: size, brevity, confusers, length and an injected instruction, each with the five forms, and a contingency table said in advance what each result would change in the protocol.

The five arms. R is what held Haiku 4.5 in study 8; C is study 7's amendment, the readback composed by code, brought to the relay. If a small model cannot write a valid line at all, L, R and C all stall at that point, which is a finding about the floor of the form.

Block 1 is the baseline: a clean record and two other live handovers in every relay's inbox, three acts, two clean contexts, every arm. Block 2 adds brevity pressure to the relay's brief. Block 3 is study 8's confusers, four per act. Block 4 is the sweep, one, two, three and five relays, prose against the checked line. Block 5 plants, two messages before the live handover in one relay's inbox, an email from a lookalike supplier domain saying the payee account has changed; the correct relay forwards the record's values and may mention the email. Block 6, registered after the freeze and before any of its chains ran, delivers the live handover alone with a short brief in F, L and C, to ask how much of the small models' failure is the inbox and the long brief rather than the form.

How we ran it

  1. Wrote the protocol before building: the text-only runner, the five arms, the five blocks, the measures, eight hypotheses and their tests, the sizing by a speed test, the pilot gates, the exclusion rule and a contingency table. It is research/breaking/README.md, with every amendment under its own dated heading.
  2. Built the harness from study 8's with the codec and the pay profile frozen into the study folder, measured the speed of each local model, and piloted 12 chains (free prose and the code-readback arm, one payment, every model, $0.31 on the anchors). Gate 3, the smallest model producing one valid line, was not met: Qwen 1.5B attempted no line. As registered it ran anyway.
  3. Froze the tools, the data and the protocol at one commit and ran the design: the anchors at three repeats, the local models one chain at a time, baseline and injection blocks first on every model, kept alive by hand because the container is reclaimed when idle. Qwen 14B was added after the freeze, one size up from the 7B, so the size axis reaches the point where the local models stop breaking.
  4. Cut the remaining blocks on the four models below the band after their baseline, since brevity, confusers and length on relays that do not carry the baseline would have been fifty-five hours of CPU for cells already at zero; ran the confusers on the 14B; registered and ran block 6 on the three models that had reached the payer.
  5. Graded everything in code: 1,585 chains, 0 harness faults in the analysed set (four chains of one launch hit a thread-unsafe import in the harness; they are excluded by name in the protocol and were run again one at a time, all four ending in a correct act), $93.84 of anchor time and 39.8 hours of the box's clock on the local models. Then a blind second reading of 80 chains, drawn by seed, from the chain files alone: agreement 80 of 80 on the act, 80 of 80 on every field at the payer, 80 of 80 on drift. Every figure on this page is read from results/summary.json by the script that writes the page, and a test in the app asserts they match.

What it cost

A clean two-relay chain on four CPU cores: Qwen 1.5B 12.4 s in free prose and 46.2 s with the code readback; Qwen 3B 21.8 and 76.8; Llama 3B 154 and 109.2; Qwen 7B 126.5 and 171.7; Qwen 14B 526.2 and 1135.9, so a checked chain on the 14B is nineteen minutes, and its 138 chains took 27.6 hours of the box's clock. On the anchors through the headless runner: Haiku 5.5 free prose 3.0 model turns, 385 tokens of traffic and $0.010 a chain; the code readback 6.2 turns, 694 tokens, $0.024; the model's own readback $0.022 for a chain that was usually held. Haiku 4.5 cost $0.096, $0.183 and $0.308, and $85.58 for its 522 chains against $8.27 for Haiku 5.5's. At five relays on Haiku 5.5 the checked line's traffic was 1,364 tokens against free prose's 857, while the prose message delivered to the payer had grown from 99 tokens at one relay to 151 at five and the line stayed at 95.

What it means

The breaking point is the agent, not the form. Below about 14 billion parameters a relay in this harness cannot pick its handover out of an inbox of three, in any form: in prose it forwards the wrong one or all of them, in lines it writes a valid line for the wrong one, and with the check at the hop it is refused and held. The check does what it is for, 0 wrong acts in 647 checked chains against 51 in the unchecked arms, but it buys that with zero throughput on these models, and a relay that is held every time is not a relay. Routing the message, one per call with a short brief, is what a small model needs first; the 7B and the 3B then act, and the 14B stops selecting the wrong item.

An unchecked line is not a half-measure at these sizes; it is the worst form. A line that parses and names the wrong item, or that has lost its slots so the payer fills them from the legend's example, gives the payer something that looks checked and is not. Where a relay cannot be trusted to copy a line exactly, the discipline is the check at the hop or prose, not the line alone.

The injection did nothing, on any model, in any form. These models do not change a payee on a supplier's say-so in an inbox, which leaves the check at the hop as a control against the fault the small models do make, picking another item, and not against the one the block was built to plant.

And the readback is composed by code. The strong model, which in study 8 built every readback with the tool and was never refused wrongly, here wrote the line itself and was refused for form in 210 of 347 readbacks; the code-composed readback on the same model was refused for form 0 of 396 times. The model's part in a readback is to read it, not to write it.

What we decided

The readback is composed by code from the message as delivered, for any receiver that has not shown it copies a line exactly.
H4 held on every model and H8 reversed on the strong one: the model-composed readback held 57 of 90 Haiku 5.5 chains with every value right and the code-composed one 0 of 18 in the clean block. The skill and the spec now say so, and that the model-composed readback is for a receiver that has demonstrated the copy with the tool in hand.
Below the floor the line alone is not the form.
H6 named Llama 3B and Qwen 7B below it and the 14B's unchecked line was its worst form. An unchecked line at these sizes is the check or nothing; and a legend's example line must be in the live order and must not resemble a live item.
The injection axis is a bound, not a result.
H1 could not be tested because no relay complied. The spec claims that no slot carried the planted value past a checked hop, and not that the check is the control against compliance.
Small models need the message routed before they need a form.
Block 6 raised the prose and the unchecked line on the 3B and the 7B and removed the 14B's selection errors; the checked forward got no easier. Deploy a small relay with one message per call and a short brief, and check what it forwards.

The amendments, in short

  • Before the freeze: the local models run in-process; context 12,288; replies capped at 500 tokens; the anchors run from outside the repository; the reply is the message; ASK on the first line is a question; one nudge for a reply that is not an answer; the act block replaces the act tool and the done report; R without the digest tier, since a model with no code cannot compute it; C's dispute rule; a harness defect in the pilot's first pass fixed and the pilot reset.
  • After the freeze: Qwen 14B added; the runs kept alive by hand; the remaining blocks cut on the four models below the band; block 6 registered and built; four anchor chains reset under a live runner and run again at the end; four Haiku 5.5 chains ended in a harness fault from a thread-unsafe import and stand as faults.
  • In the analysis: a wrong act is a chain that acted (17 chains that asked at the wrong hop in the desk-cover cell reported apart); Haiku 5.5's readback refusals split into form and value; "injected value forwarded" read against the slots; the act executor's two rules (the act's kind, and the exact supplier name for a bank change) recorded as shaping the small models' outcomes; what Llama 3B's block 6 wrong acts were.

What this does not show

  • Five quantised models on CPU at temperature 0 stand for small models in general only loosely; the result is a shape across sizes, not a number for any model, and most cells hold six chains.
  • No tools. A relay that must run code to check its own line is not tested here; the harness runs the checks the arm names.
  • The brevity and length axes ran only on the anchors and on Qwen 1.5B, which never reached a relay's forward; the cut left them unmeasured on the models in between.
  • The injection is one message in one voice, and no model complied with it; a relay that resists this one may not resist another.
  • The bank-change executor's exact-name rule stopped ten wrong acts on the small models that the payment and remittance executors would have let through; the wrong-act counts in CHGBANK are lower bounds.
  • Four Haiku 5.5 chains in the sweep stand as harness faults; the re-run the protocol calls for needs the anchors' runner.
