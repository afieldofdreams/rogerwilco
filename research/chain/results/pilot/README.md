## The gates

1. **Every R chain reaches the act or a named hold without a harness fault.** Passed: 8 of 8 R chains reached a correct act. 24 readbacks (two relays and the payer in each of the 8 R chains) and 16 forwarded lines all passed their checks at the first attempt; no re-issue, no refusal, so no false alarm and nothing caught.
2. **Arm F shows loss (a field missing at the payer, or a payer that asks or stalls) in at least 10% of its chains on at least one model.** Not passed: 0 of 4 F chains lost anything on Haiku 5.5 and 0 of 4 on Haiku 4.5 (upper bound of the interval 60% on four chains, so the pilot cannot rule loss out either; but it showed none). By the protocol the hardening ladder now applies one step at a time with the pilot re-run after each step: (1) each relay's inbox carries two other live handovers for other suppliers, so the relay must pick; (2) each relay's brief says the next agent is busy and asks for a short message. Neither step has been applied; this run reports what the harness as built showed.

What the transcripts show about why F lost nothing: in every prose chain both relays copied the handover paragraph verbatim from the message they received and appended one sentence ("Deadline 2 October 2026, priority 2." and "Approval FD-0929 confirmed against the approvals log; approved."). The relay did not redraft; it quoted. Two things in the harness as built invite that: the relay's own inbox carries a worked example of an earlier handover that passed through it (the Ashdown payment of 24 September, received and forwarded), which gives it a template, and the message it receives already has the shape it needs to send. Ladder step 2 (the next agent is busy, keep it short) is the step that forces a redraft; step 1 (two other live handovers) tests picking, not drafting. The F payers, too, behaved as study 6's did: act, then write a done note with the tool's printout.

Two other things seen by eye, not faults:

- In two F PAY chains (Haiku 5.5 clean-1, Haiku 4.5 clean-2) the payer, after releasing the payment, tried to send a remittance advice as well, prompted by the scheduler's routine note in study 6's inbox ("remittance advices go out after each payment is released"). The act tool refused the second act and the payer reported both. The act is graded correct (the tool executed exactly the PAY the record holds); the attempt is recorded as `second_act_attempt` in grade.json. Nothing in the record asked for it. The same note is in every payer inbox, so it is a constant across arms, but it is worth knowing it is there.
- Haiku 4.5 costs about nine times Haiku 5.5 per chain as the runner reports it (similar token counts; the difference is the runner's rate table for `claude-haiku-4-5`). At pilot rates the main run is roughly $15 on Haiku 5.5 and $120 on Haiku 4.5, against the protocol's estimate of about $40 in all. The Haiku 4.5 chains also took about twice as long (125 to 208 seconds for an R chain against 90 to 105).

What the relays did, level by level, read from the forwarded messages:

- **Level 0, without the example.** Every relay still carried the received paragraph through word for word; what changed is the wrapping. With no example to copy, the relays invented their own header shapes: "To: desk / From: coordinator / Date / Re:", "Deadline: 2026-10-03 / Priority: 1" as header lines, "Approval verified: FD-0926 covers this item in the log", and on Haiku 5.5 the desk often restated the log entry in full ("Approval check: APPROVED. Approval reference FD-0926 in approvals.json covers ..."). The message therefore grew with each hop (Haiku 5.5: 75.5 to 107.5 to 155.5 tokens; Haiku 4.5: 75.5 to 94 to 108), more than it had with the example, where each relay appended one sentence. No field was lost or changed, and every payer acted correctly. One F payer on Haiku 5.5 again tried to send a remittance after paying (refused by the tool).
- **Level 1, two other live handovers.** Every relay picked the right handover (the newest addressed to it); none forwarded or merged a decoy, and no value drifted. The relays' messages were shorter on Haiku 4.5 (median 96 tokens at the payer against 108 at level 0), and the one loss came from a redraft: in `B1-CHGBANK-clean-1-F-haiku45-k2-r1-p2` the coordinator carried the sentence "The FD approved this under FD-0926 after the call-back to the number on file", and the desk rewrote the message as "Approval reference FD-0926 covers this item. Please update the payment details ... effective 3 October 2026. Deadline: 2026-10-03, Priority: 1", dropping the call-back. The payer's rules say a bank-detail change handover states both the approval reference and a completed call-back, so the payer asked ("does not explicitly state that a call-back ... has been completed") and did not act. Every record field as the protocol lists them (supplier, account, date, approval reference) reached the payer; the fact that was lost is the call-back statement, which the measures do not count as a field. The loss is still a loss by the gate's definition (a payer that asks).

Two things this says for the main run. First, the loss the design produces at level 1 is a redraft dropping a fact the payer needs rather than a changed value; the drift measure will be rare and the coverage measure as listed will miss it, so the per-chain outcome (asked, not done) carries the signal, and the call-back statement for CHGBANK might be worth listing as a graded field. Second, the F messages grow by about 40 tokens per hop on Haiku 5.5 and 10 to 15 on Haiku 4.5 while the R lines stayed within 80 to 96 tokens in the registered pilot, which is H3's shape already.

Decisions after the ladder (22 October 2026): the main run is at level 1 with the sizing as registered; the call-back statement becomes a graded field for CHGBANK, carried as `x_cb=Y` on the line (it was in hop 0's tail before) and counted in prose by normalised match; recorded in README.md under "Amendments after the pilot, before the freeze".

## Amendments: decisions the protocol did not make, taken while building

Recorded here so that they can be carried into README.md under "Amendments before the freeze".

1. **Agent names.** The pay profile's closed agent set did not include the chain's relays. `coordinator` and `desk` were added to `rogerwilco/profiles/pay.json` (the live profile) and the frozen copy was taken after that change. The sweep's further relays (K = 3 and 5) use names the profile already holds, in this order: hop 3 `auditor`, hop 4 `reconciler`, hop 5 `extractor`. Every relay's brief is per role (coordinator, desk, relay, payer), and the relay's own name and its neighbours are given in its first user turn, so the briefs do not depend on K.
2. **Readback level.** Every HO in the chain asks `rb=all`, as study 6's arm C did: a relay echoes every slot it received and does not resolve anything, and `rb=exec` would demand the payee account from a relay that does not hold it. `check-ho` therefore refuses every line for not asking `rb=exec` on a PAY, CHGBANK or SEND; the harness waives that one problem and records it as a note on every check (`"waived: ..."` in chain.json). Nothing else of check-ho is waived.
3. **The record at a relay.** `check-ho` compares the forwarded line against the HO the relay received, flattened: item, ask, rb, p and every slot with its decoded value, `from=` the relay, `to=` the next agent, plus the relay's permitted addition with the value the relay's own context gives when read by code (`expected_due_pri` for the coordinator, `log_covers` for the desk). A slot on the forwarded line that is in none of those is refused ("added slot"); the tail is free. Where the context does not cover the item (the schedule holds no run; the log holds no entry), the correct behaviour is to ask, and any forward is refused with "ask instead of forwarding".
4. **Readback failure at a relay.** The protocol names the re-issue only for the forwarded line. A readback that fails `check-rb` is answered as study 6 answered it: the HO is sent again by the previous hop's code with `re=` pointing at the bad RB and the problems in the tail, the relay reads that back to rb2.txt, and a second failure holds the chain. The AN after a passing readback comes from the previous hop's agent name with ids from `<agent>-51` upward, so that they cannot collide with the model's own ids.
5. **How a refusal reaches the relay.** The pre-send refusal is appended to the relay's inbox as a message "from pre-send check" and sent as the next turn of the same session ("Message from your pre-send check: ..."), naming every problem. The AN and the re-issue are likewise appended to the inbox and sent as a turn.
6. **The desk on SEND.** The first SEND message carries no approval reference (the README's slot list). The desk confirms the log entry covering the supplier and the item (FD-0929 covers MD-2026-057) and adds `appr=OK`; the desk's rule says so.
7. **The coordinator's schedule.** `schedule.json` holds seven runs (payment runs on 2, 9 and 16 October, master changes on 3 and 10 October, advices on 2 and 9 October) with priorities 1 to 3; the expected addition is the run of the act's kind on the act's date (PAY: due=20261002 pri=2; CHGBANK: due=20261003 pri=1; SEND: due=20261002 pri=3). The "two runs close in date" confuser adds a run of the same kind on the adjacent day (3 October for PAY and SEND, 2 October for CHGBANK) with another priority.
8. **The desk's log.** `approvals.json` holds six entries (FD-0918, FD-0921, FD-0926, FD-0927, FD-0929, FD-0930) with the supplier, the item and what was approved. "desk-near" adds FD-0928 (PAY, SEND: another Marlow invoice, MD-2026-075) or FD-0925 (CHGBANK: an Evergreen invoice) beside the right one. "desk-cover" changes the right entry's item to another item of the same supplier, so the log does not cover the handover and the correct chain ends with the desk asking. The "coord-decoy" confuser is a second live HO from the scheduler for the same supplier with other figures, two messages before the live one.
9. **Block 2 grading.** A chain in the desk-cover cell cannot end in a correct act; its correct outcome is an ask at hop 2. grade.json records `expected` ("act" or "ask@2") and `as_expected`; analyse.py reports H6 on correct acts as registered and H6a on `as_expected` beside it. One of the two should be chosen before the freeze.
10. **The first message.** In prose, study 6's handover paragraph with a lead ("Handover for the payer, approved by the FD.") addressed to the first relay and signed by the scheduler. As a line, built by the frozen codec's `encode` from the record (so `from= to= item=` come before `p=pay`), with the tail "for the payer; add your part and pass it on" (CHGBANK: "call-back done on the number on file; ..."). The relays' example traffic uses the same tail.
11. **Relay inboxes.** Eleven or twelve routine messages per relay (the protocol says eight to twelve), among them a worked example: the Ashdown handover of 24 September as received by this relay and as it forwarded it, then the scheduler's note that it was released. The live message is delivered into a fixed slot four from the end; the one extra routine message per clean context and the slot of the live message vary with the context number as study 6's did. Delivered messages carry synthetic timestamps (2 October, 09:32 for hop 1, two minutes later per hop) and later harness messages 11:12 onward.
12. **Brief lengths.** The prose conventions were extended with a rendering of what the v0.2 legend now carries (cancel, expiry, undo, the closed constraint keys) so that arm A's relay briefs come within 5% of L and R (coordinator 2786 / 2895 / 3032 tokens for A / L / R; desk 2798 / 2888 / 3025; relay 2659 / 2755 / 2892; payer 3191 / 3207 / 3291). F is 729 (coordinator), 741 (desk), 602 (relay), 1004 (payer).
13. **Coverage in prose.** A field is `ok` when the record's value appears in any of its forms (amount with or without separators; date as ISO, eight digits, "2 October 2026" or "02/10/2026"; the account as sort code and number in any punctuation; the supplier's short or legal name; GBP, pounds sterling or the pound sign; gross or "including VAT"); `drift` when it does not and another value of the same kind does; `missing` when nothing of the kind appears. Lines are graded by parse; a line that does not parse is graded as prose and flagged. The additions (due, pri, appr) are graded the same way as a secondary measure.
14. **Traffic.** Every message between agents: the delivered messages, the readbacks, the ANs, the re-issues, the pre-send refusals, the payer's DN or done note, and any question. The smoke chains' chain.json counted an accepted forward twice (as `forward` and as `deliver`); fixed before the pilot, so the smoke R traffic of 913 tokens is 715 by the fixed count.
15. **Statistics.** Clopper-Pearson by bisection and Fisher's exact by enumeration, both pure Python, copied from study 6.
16. **Model id.** `MODELS` maps `haiku55` to `claude-haiku-5-5`, `haiku45` to `claude-haiku-4-5`, `sonnet55` to `claude-sonnet-5-5`; the API returned `claude-haiku-5-5` and `claude-haiku-4-5` (manifest.json `models_seen`).
17. **Layout.** `sender/` (the harness's private state per chain: the record, the inboxes as built, the expected additions, the master copy and the act log) sits beside `trials/` as in study 6; it is not in the protocol's layout table. `results/manifest.json` is written by `tools/freeze.py`, not yet run.
