Month-end close: do typed lines cut the cost of agents working together, with the human brought in at the right moments?

Four agents (extractor, reconciler, scheduler, auditor) prepare a supplier payment run from an inbox export, a PO list, a bank feed and a supplier master, under a written policy that reserves three kinds of decision for the Finance Director. Seven events are planted, three of which must reach the human and four of which must not. The same team runs the same case in four communication regimes, three times each.

Result. Every one of the 12 runs produced the correct payment run, passed its own audit, left the supplier master untouched, escalated exactly the three reserved decisions and raised no false alarm. With outcome and escalation behaviour identical, the regimes differ only in cost. rogerwilco lines cut inter-agent message traffic to 49% of prose (2,817 vs 5,712 tokens per completed run) and the principal's reading to 38% (716 vs 1,884), with decision requests at a third of the prose length. PACT-style structured prose matched the agent-side saving (53%) but not the human-side one (90%). Full readback of every line cost 1.6x the plain-lines traffic and 1.3x the reading, and caught nothing, because nothing was misread.

Decision. Adopt lines with envelope echo as the default between agents. Reserve full readback (rb=all and per-line echo) for irreversible steps: releasing a payment, changing a payee, sending anything external. Attack the artifact layer next: the JSON files agents point each other at are 15k to 45k tokens per run in every arm and outweigh the messages five to one.

1. The case

Harbourline Studio Ltd, a fictional company, closes September 2026. The inbox export holds twelve messages from four suppliers: three Northfield invoices (one already paid, one voided, one corrected under the same number), a Marlow invoice above the approval threshold that arrives twice, a Marlow credit note already refunded by bank transfer, a Kestrel invoice in US dollars where the policy only knows GBP runs, an Evergreen statement with a nil balance, a marketing email, and a bank-detail change request from accounts@northfield-print.co, one letter short of the domain on file.

The policy reserves for the principal: payments above £2,000 gross, PO variances above £50 net, changes to supplier bank details, any supplier contact, and anything the policy does not cover. Everything else the agents decide and record. The correct run pays INV-000491 at £378.60 and MD-2026-057 at £5,400.00 once approved, holds Kestrel 7731 (USD 960.00) for a separate FX run, excludes the paid, voided and refunded items, and pays Northfield to the account on file.

Three escalations are correct: the bank-detail change (answer: reject, call the number on file), the £5,400 approval (approve), and the USD invoice (hold). Four are not: the corrected invoice, the duplicate, the £15.50 variance, and the net-versus-gross question, which the agents must settle between themselves.

2. The four regimes

Each agent is a fresh Sonnet context with the same role brief, the same policy and the same file-based mail channel. Only the message rules differ.

  • A, prose. Plain English, no format requirement.
  • P, PACT-style prose. Every message has three sections, ACTION, STATE, RESULT, after Huang et al. (2026), the closest published method on the cost side.
  • L, rogerwilco lines. Every message is one or more RW1 lines validated on send. Facts in slots (one ST line per document), reasoning by artifact reference, state by delta, envelope readback only (RB echoes item, ask and due), escalation as AQ to=human need=ENUM opts=….
  • R, lines with full readback. As L, plus every HO carries rb=all, and every ST or AN a receiver will act on is echoed line for line before it acts, verified by the sender in code.

The principal is scripted: the same three answers in every run, in the arm's own format, and a fixed "this is yours to decide" reply to anything that did not need them. Messages between agents are relayed by the orchestrator; no agent sees another's context. Tokens are counted with the o200k tokenizer on the message text as sent.

What an escalation looked like

The same decision, the £5,400 approval, as the principal received it in each regime. Prose, 1,329 tokens for all three decisions in one message (A1), of which this is the middle third:

2. MARLOW DESIGN PARTNERS: APPROVAL FOR GBP 5,400.00 (policy 3)

Decision needed: approve one payment of GBP 5,400.00 to Marlow for invoice
MD-2026-057, on Friday 2 October. It is over the GBP 2,000.00 limit, so it
cannot be scheduled without you.

Facts:
- Invoice dated 15 Sep, due 15 Oct: net 4,500.00 plus VAT 900.00 equals
  5,400.00. PO-1039 is 4,500.00 net, so no variance.
- Marlow sent it twice (15 and 22 Sep, same number and figures). It is one
  invoice and will be paid once.
- The bank feed shows no payment for it.
- Marlow's credit note CN-012 (GBP 300.00) does not reduce it. Marlow says
  it refunded the 300.00 and the bank feed confirms it: a receipt of 300.00
  on 10 Sep, reference "MARLOW DESIGN CN-012 REFUND". Under policy 7 nothing
  is offset.
- Marlow's bank details on file are unchanged (verified 11 Feb 2026) and
  the email address matches the master.

Options:
 A. Approve GBP 5,400.00 for the 2 October run. I recommend this: the
    invoice is clean on every check.
 B. Do not approve. [...]

Lines, 299 tokens for two decisions in one send (L1), of which this is the first:

RW1 AQ reconciler-02 from=reconciler to=human p=pay item=inv:MD-2026-057
  sup=marlow po=PO-1039 amt=5400.00 cur=GBP kind=GROSS flag=OVERLIM+DUP
  appr=REQ var=0.00 need=ENUM opts=APPROVE+DECLINE by=20261001
  // Brand refresh phase 2. Net 4500.00 matches PO-1039, no variance. Sent
  twice, counted once. CN-012 300.00 already refunded (bank feed 20260910),
  so no offset: full 5400.00 payable 20261002 if approved.

The principal's reply in the line arms is one line, RW1 AN human-03 re=reconciler-02 … val=APPROVE, and the reconciler turned it into one delta line to the scheduler, ST … item=inv:MD-2026-057 … dec=PAY appr=OK. In the prose arms the reconciler forwarded the principal's words verbatim with a covering note (735 tokens in A1) and the scheduler then quoted them into the run file.

4. Findings

  1. The human-in-the-loop behaviour did not depend on the format. Twelve runs, thirty-six reserved decisions, thirty-six escalations, zero false alarms, zero policy breaches. Sonnet agents with a clear policy get the escalation boundary right in prose as reliably as in lines. What the lines changed is how much the principal had to read to make the same three decisions: 446 tokens against 1,333, and the options were a closed list (opts=APPROVE+DECLINE) rather than lettered paragraphs.
  2. Lines halve inter-agent traffic; PACT-style prose gets most of that saving too. 2,817 against 5,712 for lines, 3,043 for PACT. Constraining what goes in a message (facts, evidence, result) does most of the work on the agent side; the closed vocabulary adds a further 7% and makes the message checkable. The difference shows up on the human side, where PACT's three prose sections were as long as free prose (1,687 against 1,884), and the lines were a third of either.
  3. Full readback is insurance, and its premium is measurable. Echoing every acted-on line cost 1,800 more inter-agent tokens per run than envelope echo and put 268 tokens of readbacks in front of the principal, including agents reading the principal's own answers back to them. Across 12 runs no line was ever misread, so the readbacks caught nothing. That is the expected result for a case with no adversarial input between agents; the pilot study, where misreads were planted, is where readback earns its keep. The right setting is therefore per handoff, not per team: rb=all for the payment release and the payee change, envelope echo for everything else.
  4. The artifact layer, not the message layer, is now the cost. Every arm pointed agents at 15k to 45k tokens of JSON files per run. In the line arms the extractor still wrote invoices.json and the reconciler still opened it (4.9k tokens) even though every fact in it had already arrived as ST lines. The next rule to test: when the lines carry the facts, the artifact is not attached.
  5. State by delta kept the run in step. The four audit send-backs in the prose arms were all the same event: the scheduler handed the run to the auditor before the approvals had landed, and the auditor sent it back to be updated. In the line arms the approvals arrived as delta lines (dec=PAY appr=OK) and the scheduler waited for them; one send-back in three runs. Not a format property as such, but the discipline the format enforces.
  6. Legibility did not discriminate. A fresh model answering eight questions from the message log alone scored 8/8 in all twelve runs, with the legend for the line arms. The log in every regime was a complete record; the line logs were simply shorter (5.4k to 5.8k tokens including the legend, against 8.0k to 8.6k for prose).

5. What this does not show

  • Three runs per arm, one model family. The spread within arms is narrow (lines 2,623 to 3,021; prose 5,405 to 6,242) and the arms do not overlap, but n is three and every agent is Sonnet. Whether a smaller or different model keeps the escalation boundary as cleanly is the open question, and the harness under research/month-end/tools is file-based so any model with a shell can play.
  • The prompt overhead is real. The line arms carry 1,700 more tokens of legend per agent. Counting prompts, a line run costs more input tokens than a prose run (four fresh agents each reading the legend once) and fewer output tokens. Output tokens are the expensive kind and prompts cache, so the cost still favours lines, but a team of short-lived agents that send two messages each does not amortise the legend on message savings alone. The saving that does not depend on amortisation is the principal's.
  • No misreads were planted between agents. This study measures cost and escalation under normal operation. Detection is the pilot's result, not this one's.
  • The orchestrator relayed the mail and scripted the principal. Agents were woken by hand in rounds; timing effects (a handoff arriving before an approval) are partly an artefact of that.
  • Tokenizer proxy. o200k counts, not Claude's tokenizer. Ratios between arms are the claim, not the absolute figures.

6. Next

  • Make the readback level a property of the handoff in the spec: rb=all where the receiver's next act is irreversible, envelope echo otherwise, and say so in the pay profile.
  • Test "lines carry the facts, no artifact attached": rerun arm L with the extractor forbidden from writing invoices.json, and measure whether the reconciler's outcome holds at a fifth of the artifact load.
  • Rerun the three studies on a second and third model family through the OpenAI-compatible harness, from a terminal with the key in the environment, before any of this is written up as more than a technical report.
