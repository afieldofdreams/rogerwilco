# Research

Seven studies behind the protocol, each with its corpus, harness, raw logs and a results page. Everything here is synthetic; nothing comes from a client or a private knowledge graph.

| Study | Question | Folder | Results page |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wire study | Does a closed-vocabulary line beat English, JSON, glyphs or a positional form on tokens, round trip and decision parity? 240 synthetic handoffs, five formats, two models. | `wire-study/` | `wire-study/report.html` |
| Readback pilot | Does a comprehension readback catch misread briefs before the work is done, and at what cost in tokens and false alarms? 36 trials, planted faults, blind grading. | `pilot/` | `pilot/report.html` |
| Month-end close | Do typed lines cut inter-agent traffic, keep the human's read load down and bring the human in exactly when policy says, in a four-agent accounts-payable run with planted events? 28 runs: prose, PACT-style prose, disciplined prose (the same rules without the syntax), lines, lines with full readback, lines as the record (no working files, one line per source document with a `src=` pointer), lines and disciplined prose on Haiku, and an adversarial scenario with an injected instruction and a wrong answer from the principal. | `month-end/` | `month-end/results/report.html` |
| Translation | Can a clinical document become one assertion line per statement (kind, act, polarity, certainty, subject, time, values, one source pointer, the verbatim quote), completely, accurately and repeatably? 144 translations of twelve synthetic documents against 422 gold assertions: lines and JSON, Sonnet and Haiku, three runs each, every output gated on provenance. | `translation/` | `translation/report.html` |
| Readback and set-back | When a handoff before an irreversible act carries a fault, does a readback catch it before the act, at what false-alarm rate, and does the receiver's report of the act catch a wrong one? 114 trials: 19 handoffs (a payment, a payee change, a supplier email) with planted faults and clean controls, three arms (prose with no readback, prose with a model-judged readback, lines with a code-checked readback and a DN carrying the executed values), Sonnet and Haiku receivers, run as headless Claude sessions. | `readback/` | `readback/results/report.html` |
| The execution readback | Where does a readback before an irreversible act catch faults: in the sender's drafting, in transit, in the receiver's reading of its own context, or in the tool? Pre-registered. 2,762 trials: three blocks (planted faults in the handover, a correct handover in a context built to mislead, a tool that executes something other than what it was told), five arms (no readback, a prose plan readback judged by a model, an echo readback, an execution readback carrying the receiver's resolved values, a fixed-shape English block), Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5, a hardening ladder, grader validation, a partial cross-vendor run on gpt-5.5 and gpt-5.4-mini through the API harness, and a free-prose extension on Sonnet. | `plan-readback/` | `plan-readback/results/report.html` |
| Prepare and confirm at the tool | Before an irreversible act, does a readback the tool composes, a prepared transaction it states and will execute unchanged, catch what a readback the model composes does not? Pre-registered. 794 trials on Haiku 5.5 after a 66-trial pilot: a two-phase act tool (prepare, commit, abandon), four arms (no readback, the model's execution readback, the tool's statement relayed by the model, the tool's statement read by the sender with the commit enforced), three blocks (the receiver and the call, the tool with faults at prepare and at commit, the sender's draft as a regression check). On Haiku 5.5, faults the tool introduces at prepare reached a wrong act in 59 of 60 one-step trials and 0 of 60 two-phase ones, every one caught by the receiver reading the statement; no false alarm, no early commit, no altered relay. Then 1,014 trials on gpt-5.4-mini, the receiver whose readbacks fail: the echo let 14 wrong acts through, its own typed readback was held in 52 trials, the tool's statement with the commit enforced made 0 wrong acts, was held 7 times and caught the old account and the lookalike address at the sender's check; relaying the statement through that receiver was unreliable, so the sender reads the tool. | `tool-prepare/` | `tool-prepare/README.md`, site page `/research/tool-prepare` |

## Naming

The first two studies ran under the working name **formcore** with the line prefix `FC1`. The protocol was renamed **rogerwilco** with prefix `RW1` on 2026-09-29. The results pages use the new name; the raw logs, prompts and trial folders keep what the agents actually saw. Only the version token changed; no other part of the grammar did.

## Reproducing

Each folder has its own README or a docstring at the top of its harness. The wire study and the pilot were run with Claude subagents driven from a Claude session; the month-end study's harness (`month-end/tools/`) is file-based so any model that can run shell commands can play an agent; the readback study runs as headless `claude -p` sessions from `readback/run.py`; the execution readback study runs the same way from `plan-readback/tools/run.py`, as does the prepare-and-confirm study from `tool-prepare/tools/run.py`, and `plan-readback/tools/run_api.py` runs its receivers over the API harness for any `provider:model` the harness knows, with the key from the environment. `harness/` is a model-agnostic runner that drives the same scenarios over an OpenAI-compatible or Anthropic API, unattended, with keys from the environment; see its README.
